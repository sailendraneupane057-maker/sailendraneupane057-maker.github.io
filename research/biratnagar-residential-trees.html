Residents' Attitudes Towards Residential Trees in Biratnagar Metropolitan City, Nepal

Abstract

Private residential land makes up most of the area of Nepalese cities, so residents' decisions about trees on their property largely determine urban canopy cover. This study assessed the attitudes of residents of Biratnagar Metropolitan City towards trees on their property using the affective–behavioural–cognitive model of attitude. A total of 178 tree-holding households, selected through snowball sampling, were interviewed with a semi-structured questionnaire, and associations were tested with χ² and Fisher's exact tests and binary logistic regression. Almost every household planted trees for shade, air quality and food, and all reported food and shade as benefits whereas excess litter (23.6%) and reduced space (20.2%) were the main drawbacks. Overall, 93.3% rated the effect of their trees as pleasant and 70.8% intended to plant more. Intention depended on land: 96.8% of households with land left intended to plant, against 11.1% of those with none. Holding more than 10 trees raised the odds of a very pleasant rating about four-fold and of pruning about five-fold, while gender, education and residency had no significant effect. Mango and litchi dominated existing plantings, whereas ashoka, litchi, dhupi and Ficus benjamina led future preferences. Species guidance for small plots and outreach on correct pruning is recommended.
